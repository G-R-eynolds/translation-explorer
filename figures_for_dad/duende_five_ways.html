An experiment with a translation model · 24 November 2026

Duende, five ways

We asked a language model to translate one sentence into English 64 times. It does not hold one translation in mind. It holds several readings of duende, and we can find the exact point where they part and push it down the one we choose.

Cuando canta Camarón, tiene duende; me gusta mucho el duende. “When Camarón sings, he has duende; I really like the duende.”

The context makes clear which duende is meant: Lorca's, the dark power of a great performance, not the goblin of folk tales.

The model weighs five readings, and they part at one word

At that word, we can steer it into the reading we choose

At the branching point we nudge the model's internal state in the direction of one reading and let it carry on. The direction is not chosen by hand: it is worked out from the model's own translations of that reading. The rest of the sentence follows the choice by itself.

Without the context, there is only a goblin

Me gusta mucho el duende. The same 64 translations, with no context.
the elf32% the gnome18% the dwarf16% the fairy12% the goblin12% “it”11%

Alone, the sentence never reaches the artistic sense. The model still varies, but only between small creatures, which is a choice of word, not of interpretation. With Camarón in the sentence, the same word opens onto spirit, possession, charm and the dark power Lorca meant. The interpretation lives in the context more than in the word.

What was used. A small open model, Gemma 3 with 4 billion parameters, in its raw form before any chat training. It translated each sentence 64 times. The translations were grouped into readings automatically, by meaning; we read the groups and named them only from what the translations say.

What it does and does not show. These are the readings this model weighs, not every reading a person could give. Steering shows we can reach a reading reliably. It does not say which reading is right. A first try on a poem, Bécquer's Rima XXI, did not work yet: in a poem the choices are spread across many words, not decided at one.

Where this is going. A tool in which you watch a translation being written, see where it could branch, and choose the branch.
